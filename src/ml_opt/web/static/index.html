      About this experiment

      Input: permitted material volume. Target: final compliance achieved by the optimiser. Lower compliance means a stiffer structure for this load. With prescribed motion, the target is elastic work; minimising it can favour a softer structure.

      Load a problem saved from Optimisation to reuse its geometry, supports, load cases, constraints and solver settings. Only volume varies. Saved results are ignored; each case starts a fresh optimisation.

      Training fits orders 1 through the maximum you choose, up to 10. Order 1 is linear, 2 is quadratic, and 3 is cubic. Order p needs at least p + 1 training cases after the split. Inputs are scaled internally for numerical stability; displayed coefficients use the original volume.

      Test cases are kept out of fitting; their errors measure interpolation on unseen volumes. The range endpoints stay in training. All orders use the same split. The baseline always predicts the training mean.

      Higher orders can fit the training data more closely while predicting unseen cases less accurately (overfitting). Compare training and test errors. Predictions do not guarantee physical validity or generate a topology.

      Convergence means the solver met its stopping criteria, not that a global optimum was certified. Feasibility checks every active constraint. Unfinished feasible runs are excluded unless explicitly included. Cancelled, failed and infeasible runs are always excluded.

      Least-squares method reference
      About topology regression

      Input: permitted material volume. Output: one physical density per element on this fixed mesh. Each element gets its own polynomial, fitted using the training cases only.

      The saved experiment is shared with compliance regression. Training and test cases use the same split settings; endpoints stay in training. Density error is the mean absolute difference on a scale from 0 to 1.

      Predictions are clipped to [0, 1], with fixed solid and void regions preserved. Clipping does not enforce the volume target, connectivity or size constraints. The difference view compares matching volumes only.

      Check structure evaluates the predicted physical density directly, without filtering or projecting it again. For robust datasets, it checks nominal material only. It does not certify the original robust constraints. This experiment does not initialise or run optimisation.
